PLACING THE ANNUAL REPORT FOR THE YEAR 2020 OF THE
Tamil Nadu Information Commission ON THE TABLE OF THE
TAMIL NADU LEGISLATIVE ASSEMBLY.

      As per sub-section (1) of the Right to Information Act, 2005, the
State Information Commission, shall as soon as practicable after the end
of each year, prepare a report on the implementation of the provisions of
this Act during that year and forward a copy thereof to the appropriate
Government.

      2.    As per Section 25(2) of the Right to Information Act, 2005,
each Ministry or Department shall, in relation to the public authorities
within their jurisdiction, collect and provide such information to the State
Information Commission as is required to prepare the report.

      3.    Since there was delay in getting the required details from all
the Departments of Secretariat including the heads of departments
under their respective administrative control, the Tamil Nadu Information
Commission was not able to prepare the Annual Report for the year
2020 and forward it to the Government during the year 2021 itself. The
delay in placing the Annual Report for the year 2020 before the Tamil
Nadu Legislative Assembly is due to administrative reasons.

   The Right to Information Act, 2005 is one of the most empowering and
progressive legislations in independent India. It gives expression to the true
spirit of democracy which is embodied in the virtues of transparency and
accountability manifesting themselves in the various layers of governance.
The Right to Information Act, 2005, seeks to establish the principle that
“transparency is the norm and secrecy is an exception” in the working of
every public authority.      It was enacted to provide citizens with the right to
secure access to information under the control of public authorities and thus
promote transparency and accountability in the working of every public
authority. It is thus a powerful tool to curb corruption in the process of
governance.

                                    CHAPTER -2

Disclosure of information
2.1. The Right to Information is defined under the Act as access to all
     information held under the control of any public authority which is
     inclusive of right to
     (i) inspection of work, documents, records;
     (ii) taking notes, extracts or certified copies of documents or records;
     (iii) taking certified samples of material;
     (iv) obtaining information in the form of diskettes, floppies, tapes, video
           cassettes or in any other electronic mode or through printouts
           where such information is stored in a computer or in any other
           device;

                                            1
                                        1
2.2. The term public authority is defined to be any authority or body or
institution of self-government established or constituted,
(a) by or under the Constitution;
(b) by any other law made by Parliament;
(c) by any other law made by State Legislature;
(d) by notification issued or order made by the appropriate Government,
    and includes any-
     (i) body owned, controlled or substantially financed;
     (ii) non-Government organization substantially financed, directly      or
          indirectly by funds provided by the appropriate Government;

2.3. Obligations of Public Authorities
   Section 4 of the RTI Act lays down the obligations of Public Authorities for
mandatory publication of information. Section 4 (2) explicitly states that it
shall be the constant endeavour of every public authority to take steps in
accordance with the requirements of Clause (b) of sub-section (1) to provide
as much information suo motu to the public at regular intervals through
various means of communication including internet so that the public take
minimum resort to the use of this Act to obtain information.           It also
emphasizes the need to computerize as much as possible the records and
connect them through a network all over the country so that access to such
records is facilitated.

   As per Section 5 of the RTI Act, every Public Authority shall, within one
hundred days of the enactment of the Act, designate as many officers as
Public Information Officers in all administrative units or officers under it as
may be necessary to provide information to persons requesting for the
information under this Act. Every Public Authority shall also designate an
officer at each sub-divisional level or other sub-district level as Assistant
Public Information Officer to receive the applications for information or
appeals under this Act for forwarding the same forthwith to the Public
Information Officer or the Appellate Authority, as the case may be.
2.4. Three tier Mechanism under RTI Act
   A three tier Mechanism is prescribed for enforcing the Citizen’s Right to
Information. The Public Information Officer is at the first level and he/she is
responsible for providing the requested information by an RTI petitioner

                                         2
                                     2
expeditiously. The Appellate Authority is at the second level for deciding the
first appeal made to him/her by the RTI Petitioner and the State Information
Commission is at the third level to ensure the provision of the information
when a Second appeal / Complaint is filed.
2.5. Time Frame for Public Information Officers and Appellate
Authority under Public Authority
   Section 7 of the Act lays down a statutory time limit of 30 days for
disposal of requests by the Public Information Officer either by giving
information or by rejecting the same with reasons. An appeal against this lies
to the appellate authority under sub-section (1) or sub-section (2) of
Section 19 which shall be disposed of within thirty days of the receipt of the
appeal or within such extended period not exceeding a total of forty five days
from the date of filing thereof, as the case may be, for reasons to be recorded
in writing.
       However, in cases where the information sought for concerns the life
and liberty of a person, the same shall be provided within 48 hours of the
receipt of the request.
2.6. Exemption from Disclosure of Information
   Exemption from disclosure of information are covered under Section 8 of
the Right to Information Act which reads as follows:-

(1) Notwithstanding anything contained in this Act, there shall be no
      obligation to give any citizen,-
       (a) information, disclosure of which would prejudicially affect the
              sovereignty and integrity of India, the security, strategic, scientific
              or economic interests of the State, relation with foreign State or
              land to incitement of an offence;
       (b) information which has been expressly forbidden to be published by
              any court of law or tribunal or the disclosure of which may
              constitute contempt of Court;
       (c) information, the disclosure of which would cause a breach of
              privilege of Parliament or the State Legislature;
       (d) information including commercial confidence, trade secrets or
              intellectual property, the disclosure of which would harm the
                                             3
                                         3
                competitive position of a third party, unless the competent
                authority is satisfied that larger public interest warrants the
                disclosure of such information;
         (e) information available to a person in his fiduciary relationship,
                unless the competent authority is satisfied that the larger public
                interest warrants the disclosure of such information;
         (f) information received in confidence from foreign Government;
         (g) information, the disclosure of which would endanger the life or
                physical safety of any person or identify the source of information
                or assistance given in confidence for law enforcement or security
                purposes;
         (h) information which would impede the process of investigation or
                apprehension or prosecution of offenders;
         (i)    Cabinet papers including records of deliberations of the Council of
                Ministers, Secretaries and other officers:
          Provided that the decisions of Council of Ministers, the reasons thereof,
          and the material on the basis of which the decisions were taken shall
          be made public after the decision has been taken, and the matter is
          complete, or over:
          Provided further that those matters which come under the exemptions
          specified in this Section shall not be disclosed;
         (j) information which relates to personal information the disclosure of
               which has no relationship to any public activity or interest, of which
               would cause unwarranted invasion of the privacy of the individual
               unless the Central Public Information Officer or the State Public
               Information Officer or the Appellate Authority, as the case may be,
               is satisfied that the larger public interest justifies the disclosure of
               such information:
      As per Section 9 of the Right to Information Act, without prejudice to
the provisions of Section 8 of the Act, a Public Information Officer may reject
a request for information, where such a request for providing access would
involve an infringement of copyright subsisting in a person other than the
State.
      As per Section 24(4) of the Right to Information Act, 2005, nothing
contained in this Act shall apply to such intelligence and security organizations

      However, information pertaining to the allegations of corruption and

      As a clear guideline to facilitate the provision of information the RTI Act
also lays down that any information which cannot be denied to the Parliament
or State legislature shall not be denied to any person, seeking information
under the Act.
2.7. Third Party:

      Section 2 (n) of the Act defines “third party means a person other than
the Citizen making a request for information and provides that:-

      (1)     Where an appeal is preferred against an order made by the State
              Public Information Officer under Section 11 to disclose third
              party information, the appeal by the concerned third party shall
              be made within thirty days from the date of the order.
      (2)     If the decision of the State Public Information Officer against
              which an appeal is preferred relates to information of a third
              party, the State Information Commission shall give a reasonable
              opportunity of being heard to that third party.

2.8. Overriding effect
   As per Section 22 of the RTI Act, the provisions of this Act shall have
overriding effect over any other law including the Officials Secrets Act, 1923.
2.9. Powers and Functions to enforce the provision of information
   The RTI Act, 2005, prescribes the powers and functions of the Central and
State Information Commissions under Sections 18, 19 and 20 for dealing with
complaints and appeals and prescribes the powers to enforce the provision of
information to the common Citizen.

2.10. Disposal of Complaints
   The Act provides that it shall be the duty of the State Information
Commission, to receive and inquire into a complaint from any person,
      who has been unable to submit a request to a Public Information Officer
      or has been refused access to any information requested under this Act;
      or has not been given a response to a request for information or access
      to information within the time limit specified under this Act;
      or has been required to pay an amount of fee which he or she considers
      unreasonable;
      or believes that he or she has been given incomplete, misleading or
      false information under this Act; and
      or in respect of any other matter relating to requesting or obtaining
      access to records under the RTI Act.
   The State Information Commission when inquiring into any complaint
under this Section has the same powers as are vested in a Civil Court while
trying a suit under the Code of Civil procedure, 1908, in respect of the
following matters,
      (a)   Summoning and enforcing the attendance of persons and
            compel them to give oral or written evidence on oath and to
            produce the documents or things;
      (b)   requiring the discovery and inspection of documents;
      (c)   receiving evidence on affidavit;
      (d)   requisitioning any public record or copies thereof from any
            Court or office;
      (e)   issuing summons for examination of witnesses or documents;
            and
      (f)   any other matter which may be prescribed
   Further, the State Information Commission may during the inquiry of any
complaint under this Act, examine any record to which this Act applies which
is under the control of the public authority.

(1)   Any person, who does not receive a decision within the time specified
      in sub-section (1) or clause (a) of sub-section (3) of Section 7, or is
      aggrieved by a decision of the State Public Information Officer, may
      within thirty days from the expiry of such period or from the receipt of

      The appeal received shall be disposed of within thirty days of the receipt

      forty five days from the date of filing thereof, as the case may be, for
      reasons to be recorded in writing.

(2)   A second appeal against the decision shall lie within ninety days from the
      date on which the decision should have been made or was actually
      received, with the State Information Commission.

(3) In any appeal proceedings, the onus to prove that a denial of a request
      was justified shall be on the State Public Information Officer who denied

(4)    The decision of the State Information Commission shall be binding.

2.12. Penalties under Section 20 of the Act:

  (1) Where the State Information Commission at the time of deciding any
      complaint or appeal is of the opinion that the State Public Information
      Officer has without any reasonable cause, refused to receive an
      application for information or has not furnished information within the
      time specified under sub-section (1) of Section 7 or malafidely denied
      the request for information or knowingly given incorrect, incomplete or
      misleading information or destroyed information which was the subject
      of the request or obstructed in any manner in furnishing the
      information, it shall impose a penalty of two hundred and fifty rupees
      each day till application is received or information is furnished.
      However, the total amount of such penalty should not exceed twenty
      five thousand rupees.

  (2) Where the State Information Commission, at the time of deciding any
      complaint or appeal is of the opinion that the State Public Information
      Officer has, without any reasonable           cause and persistently, failed to
      receive an application for information or has not furnished information
      within the time specified under sub-section (1) of Section 7 or
      malafidely denied the request for information or knowingly given
      incorrect,        incomplete    or    misleading     information    or   destroyed
      information which was the subject of the request or obstructed in any
      manner       in    furnishing   the   information,    it   shall   recommend   for
      disciplinary action against the State Public Information Officer under
      the service rules applicable to him.

       opportunity of being heard before any penalty is imposed on him.

4.1. Power to make rules.

Section 27 of RTI Act, 2005 states that,

     (1)     The appropriate Government may, by notification in the Official
            Gazette, make rules to carry out the provisions of this Act.

    (2)      In particular, and without prejudice to the generality of the
                foregoing power, such rules may provide for all or any of the
                following matters, namely:-

          (a)     the cost of the medium or print cost price of
                 the materials to be disseminated under sub-section (4) of
                 Section 4;

          (b) the fee payable under sub-section (1) of Section 6;

          (c) the fee payable under sub-section (1) and (5) of Section 7;

           (d) the salaries and allowances payable to and the terms and
                  conditions of service of the officers and other employees under
                  sub section (6) of Section 16;

          (e)      the    procedure     to        be   adopted   by   the      Central
                  Information Commission or State Information commission, as
                  the case may be, in deciding the appeals under sub-section (10)
                  of Section 19; and

           (f) any other matter which is required to be, or may be prescribed.


Section 28 of RTI Act, 2005 also states that,

      (1)       The competent authority may, by notification in the Official
                Gazette, make rules to carry out the provisions of this Act.

      (2) In particular, and without prejudice to the generality of the
                foregoing power, such rules may provide for all or any of the




                                             16
                                         16
              following matters, namely:-

              (i) the cost of the medium or print cost price of the materials to
                     be disseminated under sub-section (4) of Section 4;

         (ii) the fee payable under sub-section (1) of Section 6:

             (iii) the fee payable under sub-section (1) of section 7; and
             (iv) any other matter which is required to be, or may be,
                     prescribed.

“Competent Authority” is defined under the Act to include

             (i)       the Speaker in the case of the House of the people or the
                       Legislative Assembly of a State or a Union territory having
                       such Assembly and the Chairman in the case of the Council
                       of States or Legislative Council of a State;

              (ii)     the Chief Justice of India in the case of the Supreme Court;

              (ii)     the Chief Justice of the High Court in the case of a
                       High Court;

              (iv) the President or the Governor, as the case may be, in the
                       case of other authorities established or constituted by or
                       under the Constitution;

              (v)     the   administrator        appointed   under Article 239 of     the
                      Constitution;

Recommendations of the Commission to the Government
  1. Section 25(2) of the RTI Act, 2005 mandates that each Ministry or
    Department shall in relation to the Public authorities within their
    jurisdiction,   collect    and      provide    such   information   to   the    State
    Information Commission, as is required to prepare the Annual Report.
    But it has been found that most of Departments submit their reports
    belatedly, which in turn makes further delay in preparation and
    submission of the Annual Report to the Government in time. The
    Personnel and Administrative Reforms Department which is the nodal
    department on RTI is to arrange to issue necessary instructions to all
    the Departments of Secretariat to strictly adopt a procedure to submit
    their reports pertaining to the previous year within three months of the
    next year.

  2. Most of the RTI petitioners seek information which should have been
    proactively made public without the petitioners resorting to file RTI
    petitions. Inspite of provision under Section 4(1)(b) of the RTI Act, 2005
    for suo motu disclosure of information on 17 items, non adherence of
    the said provision in letter and spirit by the Public Authorities, has
    resulted in forcing the public to file RTI petitions for information. It is,
    therefore, suggested that instructions be issued to all the Public
    Authorities for strict compliance of Section 4(1)(b) of the RTI Act, 2005.
  3. In a large number of cases, the Public Information Officers express their
    inability to provide the required information to the applicants due to
    non-availability of the related records. Strengthening the Record
    Management System in the various Departments of the Government is
    necessary to mitigate the above problem.
  4. It is observed that many of the Public Information Officers and first
    Appellate    Authorities    still    have     inadequate   understanding       of   the
    provisions of the RTI Act, 2005. Sensitization Programmes on RTI Act
    need to be organized by the Public Authorities periodically so as to make
    the implementation of the provisions of the Act more effective.
                                            35
                                          35
5. There is an urgent need to improve the level of awareness among the
  public in rural areas so that the benefits of the Right to Information Act
  will reach them for which awareness campaigns need to be organized by
  the Government.
6. There is need for more awareness among the common people about the
  purpose and outcome of the Act. A large number of people are of the
  view that the Act is to be used as a grievance redressal mechanism and
  that the Information Commission has the authority to give directions to
  the Executive on redressing grievances like issue of ration card,
  providing house service connection, payment of pension and so on.
7. It is noticed that while opening the Government of Tamil Nadu website,
  several important public interest Government Orders, Acts and rules,
  Gazette Notifications and other important Government letters have not
  been uploaded in most departments. It is therefore, requested to upload
  the above important Orders, Acts etc., on to the Government of Tamil
  Nadu website at regular intervals.
8. Similarly, it is also noticed that Heads of Departments and others
  (Commissionerates/Directorates,                Undertakings,          Corporations,
  Universities, Institutions) have also not uploaded the important rules,
  letters, orders, Acts etc. on their websites. This area of work requires
  special attention for which it is requested that necessary Government
  instructions may be given.
9. In the case of few departments official websites have not yet been
  created. The Head of Departments may ensure that the official websites
  are created without further delay, for which necessary Government
  instructions may be given immediately.
10. Physical appearance of Petitioner and PIO should be on the discretion of
  the   Commission    Under    Rule    7    of    the   Tamil    Nadu    Information
  Commission (Appeal Procedure) Rules, 2012, it has been laid down that
  the appellant or the complainant shall be present in person at the time
  of hearing by the Commission.            After the onset of Covid-19, it has
  become necessary to relax this rule and hear the persons concerned
  through the virtual mode.           It is, therefore, suggested that the

                                      36
                                 36
  Tamil Nadu Information Commission (Appeal Procedure) rules may be
  modified so as to allow for the Commission to decide at its discretion
  whether the physical presence of the complainant or appellant is
  necessary. The Commission may dispense with the physical presence of
  the individual at the time of hearing and instead choose to hear the
  parties concerned through the virtual mode.
11. Commission may conduct enquires in the virtual mode (audio/ video)
  for which the petitioners and Public Information Officers shall identify
  themselves to the satisfaction of the Commission.
       The Tamil Nadu Information Commission (Appeal Procedure) Rules
  2012, at present, do not make any mention of hearing the parties
  through the virtual mode. After the onset of Covid-19, the Supreme
  Court and High Courts have also at their discretion switched over to the
  practice of hearing the parties through the virtual mode. Many of the
  Information Commissions in the country are now adopting the audio
  mode for hearing the parties concerned and in this respect, the Tamil
  Nadu Information Commission was the first in the country to switch over
  to the audio mode to ensure that the RTI petitions are disposed of
  expeditiously even during the times of lockdown.                Therefore, it is
  suggested   that   a   provision   should   be   specifically    made    in   the
  Tamil Nadu Information Commission (Appeal Procedure) rules to permit
  hearings by adopting audio or visual mode at the discretion of the
  Commission.
12. Even for payment of fee for photocopies of documents, Court fee stamp
  may be allowed.
      Under the Tamil Nadu Right to Information (Fees) Rules, 2005,
  provision has been made for paying the sum of Rs.10/- to accompany
  every application for obtaining information, in the form of a Court fee
  stamp for affixing it with the application. However, in G.O.Ms.No.989,
  Public (Estt.1 & Leg) Department, dated 07.10.2005, it has been
  ordered that for copies of documents and for inspection of records,
  necessary payment to be made only through a Demand Draft or
  Bankers Cheque or by way of Cash. Since, cash is not accepted in most

                                     37
                                 37
departments of the Government now and obtaining a demand draft or
bankers cheque leads to a lot of harassment for the common public,
(since it is difficult to obtain bankers cheque or demand draft for small
amounts), it is suggested that a provision may be made for charges
payable for copies of documents and for inspection of records to be paid
in the form of Court fee stamps which are widely available.

     ORDER:
           The Government of India have enacted the Right to Information Act,
     2005 (Central Act 22 of 2005) to provide for setting out a practical regime
     system of right to information for citizens to secure access to information under
     the control of public authorities, in order to promote transparency and
     accountability in the working of every public authority.
            2. In the Government letter Ms second read above, the Government
     have issued orders designating Public Information Officers and Appellate
     Authorities for implementation of the said Act in all public authorities. As per
     section 27 of the said Act, the appropriate Government may, by notification in
     the Official Gazette, make rules to carry out the provisions of the said Act.
     Accordingly, the following Notification, shall be published in the Tamil Nadu
     Government Gazette Extraordinary, dated the Friday, 7th October, 2005:-
                                   NOTIFICATION
           In exercise of the powers conferred by section 27 of the Right to
     Information Act, 2005 (Central Act 22 of 2005), the Governor of Tamil Nadu
     hereby makes the following Rules:-
                                 RULES
     1) Short title.- These rules may be called the Tamil Nadu Right to Information
     (Fees) Rules, 2005.
     2) Definitions.- In these Rules, unless the context otherwise requires,-

           (a) “Act” means the Right to Information Act, 2005 (Central Act 22 of
               2005);

           (b) Words and expressions used in these Rules and not defined shall
               have the same meaning assigned to them in the Act.




                                            66
      3) FEES;

             (a) Every application for obtaining information under sub-section (1) of
                 section 6 of the Act shall be accompanied by an application fee of
                 rupees fifty by cash or by demand draft or bankers cheque payable in
                 the head of account, as may be specified by the Public Authority.
             (b) For providing the information under sub-section (1) of section 7 of the
                 Act, a fee shall be charged by way of cash against proper receipt or
                 by demand draft or banker’s cheque payable in the head of account,
                 as specified by Public Authority at the following rates:-
                   (i)     rupees two for each page (in A-4 or A-3 size paper)
                           createdor copied;
[


                   (ii)    actual charge or cost price of a copy in larger size paper;
                   (iii)   actual cost or price for samples or models; and
                   (iv)    for inspection of records, no fee for the first hour, and a fee
                           of rupees five for each fifteen minutes (or fraction thereof)
                           thereafter.
             (c) For providing the information under sub-section (5) of section 7, the
             fee shall be charged by way of cash against proper receipt or by demand
             draft or banker’s cheque payable in the head of account, as specified by
             the Public Authority at the following rates:-
                   (i)     for information provided in diskette or floppy rupees fifty
                           perdiskette or floppy; and
                   (ii)    for information provided in printed form at the price fixed
